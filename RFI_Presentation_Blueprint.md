## 2. MAIN GOAL

> **To secure immediate decisions and formal instructions from the design consultant and sub-contractors on the three critical open RFIs — preventing programme delay and budget overrun across 14 affected floors.**

This presentation is **action-oriented**, not informational. Every slide must move the audience closer to a decision, an approval, or an assigned action. It is not a passive status update — it is a structured escalation.

### Secondary Goals
- Surface the systemic MEP–Civil coordination gap before it reaches upper floors (13F+)
- Establish a shared priority action register with named owners and deadlines
- Align all stakeholders on the ID coordination bottleneck (present in 52% of RFIs)

---

## 3. TARGET AUDIENCE

### Primary Audience (Decision-Makers)
| Role | Interest | What They Need to Leave With |
|---|---|---|
| **Client / Employer's Representative** | Budget protection, programme adherence | Awareness of 3 critical blocks; confidence in management response |
| **Design Consultant / Lead Engineer** | Technical sign-off authority | Specific questions requiring formal instructions within defined deadlines |
| **Structural Engineer** | Beam, slab, drop panel approvals | Confirmation of sleeve details, reinforcement specs, DM submission impact |

### Secondary Audience (Implementers)
| Role | Interest | What They Need to Leave With |
|---|---|---|
| **MEP Sub-contractor** | Workfront clarity, avoiding rework | Clear scope of re-submissions and verification reports required |
| **Main Contractor's PM** | Programme protection | Priority action register with owners and dates |
| **Interior Design (ID) Coordinator** | Coordination instruction | Formal list of 14 RFIs awaiting ID sign-off |
| **Tiling Sub-contractor** | Tile layout finality | Understanding that floor drain locations depend on their approved tile layout |

### Communication Style Calibration
- **Avoid jargon overload** — the Client rep may not be a technical engineer
- **Lead with risk and impact** — engineers respond to data; clients respond to cost and time
- **Frame as solutions, not problems** — every issue slide must end with a defined resolution path

---

## 4. KEY MESSAGE (The Single Throughline)

> **"Three unresolved RFIs are actively blocking four floors of construction. We need formal decisions today — not next week."**

Every slide should connect back to this throughline. The audience should feel mild urgency without alarm — the message is: *"We have identified the risks early and have a clear resolution plan. We need your sign-off."*

### Supporting Messages (Tier 2)
1. A systemic MEP–Civil clash detection gap exists — it must be fixed before upper floors
2. 52% of open RFIs are waiting on Interior Design coordination — a single coordinator can unblock them
3. The language and quality of RFI submissions can be significantly improved to shorten consultant response cycles

## 5. SLIDE BLUEPRINT — FULL FLOW

### PHASE 1: ORIENT (Slides 1–3) — *"Here is where we are"*
**Goal:** Establish context, credibility, and shared understanding in under 5 minutes.

---

**SLIDE 1 — Title / Cover**
- Presentation title, project reference, date, classification
- Visual: Full-bleed dark background with project/company branding
- No bullet points
- Sets professional tone immediately

---

**SLIDE 2 — The One Thing**
- Single bold statement: *"3 RFIs are blocking 4 floors. We need decisions today."*
- No table, no data — one sentence
- Visual: bold typography against clean background
- Purpose: anchor the audience's attention before the detail arrives

---

**SLIDE 3 — RFI Dashboard (KPI Overview)**
- 8 KPI tiles: Total RFIs, Critical/Open, Rejected, Floors Affected, Civil RFIs, MEP RFIs, ID Coord Needed, Max Delay Risk
- 4 dominant clash themes (shaft undersizing, beam penetrations, drop panel clashes, ID coordination gap)
- Visual: colour-coded metric tiles in grid layout
- Talking point: *"This is the full picture in 30 seconds. We'll unpack the critical items now."*

---

### PHASE 2: DIAGNOSE (Slides 4–8) — *"Here is what is wrong and why it matters"*
**Goal:** Walk the decision-makers through the three critical RFIs with precision. Each gets a dedicated slide. No more than 10 minutes total for this phase.

---

**SLIDE 4 — Critical RFI #1: ARC_042 + ARC_054**
- Issue: Beam L7-B-08 must shift 500mm; shaft enlarged to 1,200×450mm
- Status: Design Discussion On-going (red alert banner)
- Impact: 4 floors blocked (7F, 8F, 9F, 10F)
- Delay estimate: 3–6 weeks if unresolved
- Ends with: *"We need a formal structural instruction today."*
- Visual: Two-column layout — left: issue detail; right: risk metrics + action checklist

---

**SLIDE 5 — Critical RFI #2: ARC_056**
- Issue: Pipe-through-beam proposal — formally rejected
- Status: Not Accepted (both points)
- Required: 6" sleeve + structural reinforcement detail + re-submission within 7 days
- Systemic note: Same error found in ARC_057, ARC_058, ARC_059 — pattern, not isolated
- Visual: Before/After comparison of rejected vs. required approach

---

**SLIDE 6 — Critical RFI #3: ARC_058**
- Issue: Multiple large pipes through structural drop panel
- Status: Conditional — pending 4-document simultaneous package
- Bottleneck: Tile contractor + Structural Engineer + MEP contractor must submit together
- Repeat risk: Same scenario at 7F, 8F, 11F, 12F
- Visual: Dependency diagram showing 3-party submission requirement

---

**SLIDE 7 — The Systemic Pattern (Root Cause)**
- Why are these clashes happening? Not isolated mistakes — a coordination system gap
- 3 root causes: No clash detection before construction; MEP shop drawings not cross-referenced with structural; No multi-trade coordination meeting cadence
- Visual: Simple fishbone/cause diagram or 3-column card layout
- Talking point: *"If we only solve these three RFIs without fixing the system, the same clashes will appear at 13F, 14F, and above."*

---

**SLIDE 8 — The ID Coordination Bottleneck**
- 14 of 27 RFIs (52%) are waiting on Interior Design sign-off
- Visual: Horizontal bar chart showing RFI count by blocking category
- Impact: Until ID confirms, MEP cannot finalise drain positions, FT locations, shaft layouts
- Resolution: Appoint a dedicated MEP–ID coordinator; weekly close-out register
- Talking point: *"One person with a weekly meeting could unblock half our RFI log."*

## 7. VISUAL DESIGN PRINCIPLES

### Colour System
| Colour | Use Case |
|---|---|
| **Navy (#0D2B55)** | Headers, title bars, primary structure |
| **Steel Blue (#1B4F72)** | Section dividers, sub-headers, footer |
| **Red (#E84C3C)** | Critical/P1 items, rejected status, HIGH risk |
| **Amber (#F39C12)** | Medium risk, warnings, on-going items |
| **Green (#1E8756)** | Approved, LOW risk, completed actions |
| **Light Grey (#F4F6F7)** | Slide backgrounds, alternating table rows |
| **White (#FFFFFF)** | Text on dark backgrounds, clean card surfaces |

### Typography Rules
- **Slide titles:** 24–28pt, bold, white on navy bar
- **Body text / table content:** 8–11pt, dark grey, no all-caps
- **KPI numbers:** 24–36pt, bold, white on coloured tile
- **Avoid:** Comic Sans, decorative fonts, more than 2 typefaces

### Layout Principles
- Every slide has one dominant visual element (table, KPI grid, card set, or chart)
- Maximum 3 bullet points per slide — prefer visual formats over lists
- Every slide has the same footer: Project | Presentation Title | Date | Confidential
- Use icons sparingly — only where they replace a label, not decorate it

## 8. SPEAKER NOTES STRUCTURE (Per Slide Type)

Each slide should have speaker notes built around three anchors:

```
HOOK:      One sentence to open the slide — frame what the audience is about to see.
FACT:      The two or three most important data points — deliverable verbally.
BRIDGE:    How this slide connects to the next — maintains narrative flow.
```

### Example — Slide 4 (Critical RFI #1)
> **HOOK:** "This is the single most important item in today's briefing."
> **FACT:** "Beam L7-B-08 must physically move 500mm. That is a structural re-design, not a site fix. Four floors cannot pour until this is resolved in writing."
> **BRIDGE:** "And this isn't the only rejection — the next slide shows a second formal consultant rejection that compounds this risk."
