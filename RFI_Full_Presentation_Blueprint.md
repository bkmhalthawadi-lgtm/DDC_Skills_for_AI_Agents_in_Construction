## Topic: RFI (Request for Information) Management in Construction
### Civil & MEP Discipline — Executive Briefing | Project P159

> **Assumption:** The topic is the RFI log analysis for Project P159, covering Civil and MEP discipline clashes, critical unresolved items, and the resolution roadmap. The audience includes project decision-makers and technical leads. Adjustments are noted where scope may vary.

---

## STEP 1 — PRESENTATION PURPOSE

### Main Goal
> **To convert a backlog of unresolved RFIs into a clear, time-bound decision agenda — securing formal consultant instructions that protect the project programme and prevent cost overruns.**

### What the Audience Should Walk Away With

| Outcome Type | Specific Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Understand** | Which RFIs are critical, why they are blocked, and what the cascading programme risk is if left open |
| **Believe** | That the project team has diagnosed the root causes, not just the symptoms — and has a credible resolution plan |
| **Feel** | Urgency without alarm — confident the team is in control; aware that delay in decisions has direct cost consequences |
| **Do** | Issue formal instructions, approve re-submissions, schedule the multi-party coordination meeting, and assign the MEP–ID coordinator — all within the week |

## STEP 2 — TARGET AUDIENCE

### Primary Audience Profile

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Who they are** | Client / Employer's Representative, Lead Design Consultant, Structural Engineer |
| **Knowledge level** | Technically literate — familiar with RFI processes but may not know every detail of this specific log |
| **Top priority** | Programme protection and cost certainty |
| **Biggest concern** | Being handed a problem without a solution, or being asked to sign off without adequate information |
| **Decision authority** | Full authority to issue instructions, approve structural details, and approve re-submissions |
| **Time availability** | Time-pressured — will disengage if the presentation loses pace after 5 minutes |

### Secondary Audience Profile

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Who they are** | Main Contractor's PM, MEP Sub-contractor, Interior Design Coordinator, Tiling Contractor |
| **Knowledge level** | Deep technical knowledge in their own discipline; limited visibility across disciplines |
| **Top priority** | Clarity on what they are responsible for and by when |
| **Biggest concern** | Being assigned actions they cannot execute due to dependencies on others |
| **Role in the meeting** | Receive, acknowledge, and commit to priority actions |

### Tone & Depth Calibration

- **Tone:** Authoritative, factual, calm — solution-oriented rather than blame-oriented
- **Depth:** Lead with impact (cost, schedule), follow with technical specifics — never the reverse
- **Language:** Avoid passive constructions. Use direct language: "This is blocked," "This requires your sign-off today," not "It may be worth considering..."
- **Pace:** 45 seconds per KPI, 2–3 minutes per critical RFI, 1.5 minutes per table row group

## STEP 3 — CORE MESSAGE

### Anchor Message (One Sentence)
> **"Three open RFIs are blocking four floors of active construction — and without formal decisions this week, the programme cannot recover without cost consequences."**

### Three Supporting Ideas

**Supporting Idea 1 — Scale of Impact**
> The RFI log is not routine administration. Of 27 RFIs, 3 are programme-critical, 14 depend on Interior Design sign-off, and 6 floors face slab-pour delays. The cumulative schedule risk is 4–8 weeks if the pattern is not broken now.

**Supporting Idea 2 — Root Cause, Not Isolated Events**
> The beam penetration rejections (ARC_056, ARC_057, ARC_058) are not isolated mistakes — they reveal that MEP shop drawings were never cross-referenced with structural drawings before construction began. Fixing the three RFIs without fixing the process will produce the same clashes at every floor above 12F.

**Supporting Idea 3 — Resolution Is Achievable This Week**
> Every action required to close the P1 items can be initiated within 3–5 working days. The barrier is not complexity — it is the absence of a formal instruction. With today's decisions, the programme is recoverable.

## STEP 4 — IDEAL DECK SIZE

### Recommendation: **14 Slides**

| Rationale | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Audience attention span** | Senior technical and client stakeholders in a construction briefing engage fully for 25–35 minutes. 14 slides at an average of 2 minutes each fits this window precisely. |
| **Content density** | The topic contains three layers: context, technical detail, and action. These cannot be compressed below 12 slides without losing the persuasive architecture. They should not exceed 16 without adding cognitive load. |
| **Decision density** | 3 of the 14 slides are direct decision slides. Keeping them separated by context slides gives the audience time to process before being asked to commit. |
| **Leave-behind value** | At 14 slides, the PDF export is a complete document — executives can circulate it without an accompanying explanation. Fewer slides leave gaps; more slides become a report, not a deck. |

> **Rule of thumb applied:** One slide per distinct idea. If two ideas belong on the same slide, they are the same idea. If a slide has no decision, data point, or narrative function, cut it.

### SLIDE 7 — Critical RFI #3: ARC_CRC_KA_058

**Objective:** Expose the multi-party coordination bottleneck and propose a single structural intervention (a coordination meeting) that unblocks it.

**Key Points:**
- Issue: Multiple large pipes (Ø160 SP, Ø110 VP, Ø160 WP) must penetrate a structural drop panel — the most sensitive zone in a post-tensioned flat plate
- Condition: Consultant approval is conditional on a simultaneous 4-document submission from three separate parties
- Four required documents: tile layout (tiling contractor), edge distances (structural engineer), sleeve reinforcement detail (structural engineer), final MEP pipe routing (MEP sub-contractor)
- Repeat risk: Same scenario at 7F, 8F, 11F, 12F — if not addressed as a batch, it repeats six times
- Delay estimate: 4–8 weeks if coordination is not initiated immediately

**Suggested Visual / Layout:**

**Speaker Note / Delivery Guidance:**
> Frame the dependency clearly: *"This is not a technical problem — it is a coordination problem. All four documents are producible. ### SLIDE 8 — The Root Cause (Why These Clashes Keep Happening)

**Objective:** Shift the conversation from individual RFIs to the systemic gap — and pre-empt the same problem at upper floors. This is the most strategic slide in the deck.

**Key Points:**
- Root Cause 1: MEP shop drawings were not cross-referenced with structural drawings before construction commenced
- Root Cause 2: No formal clash-detection review (BIM/Navisworks or manual overlay) was conducted at pre-construction stage
- Root Cause 3: No standing multi-trade coordination meeting cadence — issues surface reactively via RFIs instead of being prevented proactively
- Forward risk: Floors 13F and above will repeat the same pattern unless the coordination process changes now
- Proposed fix: One BIM clash-detection workshop before works above 12F commence

**Suggested Visual / Layout:**
- Three-column card layout, each card headed with a root cause label and a short explanatory paragraph
- Below the three cards: a horizontal arrow labelled "What happens if we don't fix this" pointing to a red box: "Same clashes repeated on every remaining floor"
- Clean, analytical look — no icons, no decorative elements

**Speaker Note / Delivery Guidance:**
> This is the slide where you earn long-term credibility. Say: *"We are not here just to close these three RFIs. We are here to prevent them recurring. A single clash-detection workshop — one day of the right people in a room with the BIM model — would eliminate the majority of what we are dealing with today before it becomes an RFI at all. We will propose that in the action register."*

### SLIDE 9 — The Interior Design Bottleneck

**Objective:** Quantify and legitimise a coordination gap that is often treated informally — and propose a specific, low-cost structural fix.

**Key Points:**
- 14 of 27 RFIs (52%) are blocked pending Interior Design sub-contractor sign-off
- ID approval is a prerequisite for finalising: floor drain positions, floor trap locations, shaft dimensions in all wet areas
- Current state: No dedicated MEP–ID coordination function; issues are chased informally, inconsistently
- Consequence: MEP sub-contractor cannot finalise any wet-area layouts until ID confirms
- Proposed fix: Appoint a named MEP–ID Coordinator with authority to schedule and minute a weekly 60-minute close-out meeting

**Suggested Visual / Layout:**
- Horizontal bar chart showing RFI count by blocking category (waiting on ID / waiting on consultant / waiting on structural / waiting on tiling)
- Pull-quote callout: *"52% of open RFIs have the same solution: one coordinator and a weekly meeting."*
- Simple before/after comparison: "Current State (informal, reactive)" vs. "Proposed State (named coordinator, weekly register)"

**Speaker Note / Delivery Guidance:**
> Keep this slide brief — the finding is striking enough to land without elaboration. Say: *"More than half the items in this log are not technically difficult. They are coordination-difficult. One person with a weekly meeting and a close-out register would unblock them faster than any technical review."* Then pause and ask: *"Does anyone in the room want to nominate that person today?"*

## STEP 6 — ENGAGEMENT RECOMMENDATIONS

### Storytelling Technique: The Cost of Inaction Frame
Open the diagnostic section (Slides 5–7) with a consistent frame: *"Here is what is blocked. Here is what it costs per week it stays blocked. Here is what closes it."* This three-beat pattern is more persuasive than a technical description because it forces the audience to weigh inaction as an active cost.

### Data Placement Strategy
| Where to Use Data | What Type of Data | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Slide 4 (Dashboard) | KPI numbers, floor counts | Establish scale and credibility immediately |
| Slides 5–7 (Critical RFIs) | Delay weeks, cost risk ratings | Create urgency around specific decisions |
| Slides 10–11 (Tables) | Full RFI log data | Provide reference depth for technical audience |
| Slide 12 (Action Register) | Target dates, owner names | Convert analysis into accountability |

### Audience Interaction Points
| Slide | Interaction Type | How to Execute |
|---|---|---|
| **Slide 5** | Direct question | "Can we get a verbal commitment on the instruction today?" |
| **Slide 7** | Commitment request | "Can we schedule the 90-minute coordination meeting before we leave?" |
| **Slide 9** | Nomination question | "Who would like to nominate the MEP–ID Coordinator?" |
| **Slide 12** | Live agreement | Read each P1 item aloud; get verbal confirmation from the named owner |
| **Slide 14** | Closing challenge | "Who wants to go first?" — silence until someone speaks |

### Persuasive Framing Techniques
- **Loss aversion framing** (Slide 5): Frame the delay risk as cost already accumulating, not a future possibility — *"Every week ARC_042 stays open is a week of slab programme lost."*
- **Social proof** (Slide 8): *"BIM clash detection is standard practice on comparable projects — it prevents 70–80% of coordination RFIs from ever being raised."*
- **Specificity creates credibility** (Slide 6): Quoting the consultant's exact rejection language (*"NOT ACCEPTED"*) is more powerful than paraphrasing it. It shows the team has read the RFI log carefully and is not generalising.
- **Contrast** (Slide 9): The before/after comparison (informal coordination vs. weekly close-out register) makes the solution feel proportionate and easy to approve.

### Transitions Between Sections
- **Orient → Diagnose:** *"You now have the full picture. Let me take you inside the three items that demand decisions today."*
- **Diagnose → Prescribe:** *"Now that we have established what is wrong and why, here is the complete RFI inventory and the action plan we are proposing."*
- **Prescribe → Decide:** *"Everything you have seen in the last ten minutes points to seven actions. Three of them need to be agreed in this room, right now."*

### Where to Include Comparisons or Case Studies
- **Slide 8 (Root Cause):** Reference a comparable project where a pre-construction BIM workshop eliminated 80% of structural-MEP clashes — makes the recommendation feel proven, not theoretical
- **Slide 9 (ID Bottleneck):** Reference the time cost of informal coordination vs. a structured close-out meeting (e.g., *"Industry data shows informal coordination cycles take 3–5x longer to close than a structured weekly register"*)
